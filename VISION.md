# Vision

## Make tumor evidence usable where specialist support is scarce

Sequencing can identify changes in a tumor. Interpreting those findings takes clinical expertise, time and access to evidence that is spread across different sources. Many hospitals do not have a molecular tumor board to do that work.

OpenOncology aims to bring that evidence into one report that an oncologist can inspect. Each candidate drug should come with its source, evidence context and the information still missing. The clinician remains responsible for interpreting the report and deciding what it means for the patient.

## Start with the lab's interpretation workflow

Our first target customers are sequencing labs that already produce genomic reports but need oncology interpretation support. Hospitals without molecular tumor boards are the next segment. Initial clinical evaluation is planned in Nepal.

The code is open source so an institution can inspect and run it on infrastructure it controls. The planned commercial offering is hosted access with support and validation at each site. The current repository is a research prototype; the hosted offering and clinical validation are still ahead.

## Put access beside evidence

A recommendation is of limited use if the patient cannot obtain or afford the drug. We plan to add local availability, access and affordability information beside the clinical evidence. This layer is not implemented today.

## Earn the next stage through evaluation

The next priorities are a hosted evaluation workspace, reliable lab integration and independent clinical review. Patient-wide ranking, alteration handling and treatment context need further integration and evaluation before deployment.

Retrospective results help identify gaps, but they do not establish treatment benefit or readiness for clinical use. The [evaluation reference](docs/BENCHMARK.md) explains what has been measured. The [clinical-use roadmap](docs/ROADMAP_TO_CLINICAL_USE.md) records the remaining requirements.

## Keep research separate from the initial offering

Multi-omics, federated learning and mobile access are longer-term research directions. The repository also contains discovery briefs, marketplace and crowdfunding experiments from a broader research agenda. Those modules are outside the initial lab offering and do not establish a custom-manufacturing or patient-treatment service.
